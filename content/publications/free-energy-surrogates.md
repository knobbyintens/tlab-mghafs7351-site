Learned free-energy surrogates promise to accelerate molecular simulations, yet their thermodynamic consistency remains poorly characterized. We introduce a coarse-grained benchmark spanning three organic liquids and show that neural surrogates trained on short trajectories reproduce pair-correlation functions within two percent error while drifting in mean potential energy by up to forty kilojoules per mole over nanosecond horizons. The drift originates in an unconstrained entropy term and is not removed by longer training, which motivates explicit thermodynamic constraints.
